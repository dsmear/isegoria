\section{Derivations}\label{app:proofs}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\Cref{prop:leak}}{The majoritarian-leak proposition} step by step}

Under (T1), subtracting the fitted camp equations gives
$(\varphi_B-\varphi_A)f_j=2d_j$, hence $f_j=d_j/h$. Averaging gives
$\mu+\iota_j+cf_j=q_j$, hence $\iota_j=q_j-\mu-t d_j$ with $t=c/h$.
The raw camp means differ by the explicitly defined residuals; exact raw reproduction is
incompatible with a nondegenerate stationary point in the complete, uniformly weighted case.

The reviewer positions sum to $\sum_uf_u=nh(t+\kappa)$. Therefore
\eqref{eq:gauge-rev} reads
$\lambda_b(C-tS)=\lambda_fnh^2(t+\kappa)$, with
$S=\sum_jd_j^2$ and $C=\sum_jd_j(q_j-\mu)$. This solves to
$t=(\lambda_b C-\lambda_fnh^2\kappa)/(\lambda_bS+\lambda_fnh^2)$.
Substitution into the item intercept gives \eqref{eq:leak}. Substituting
$q_j=\mbar_j-\bar e_j$ and $d_j=\Delta_j-e_{\Delta j}$ then gives the exact difference
$-\bar e_j-\ell\kappa e_{\Delta j}-(\lambda_bC/D)d_j$ used in \eqref{eq:leak-error}.

When $C=0$, $t=-\ell\kappa\in[-\kappa,0]$. Equation~\eqref{eq:scale} gives
$nh^2(1+t^2+2\kappa t)=S/h^2$ and hence
$\rho=(\lambda_b/\lambda_f)\sqrt{1+t^2+2\kappa t}\sqrt{S/n}$.
The square-root factor lies in $[\sqrt{1-\kappa^2},1]$. This is a fixed-point
characterization on the fitted quantities. Replacing that factor by 1 incurs at most its
stated multiplicative range (0.98--1 at $\kappa=0.2$); replacing fitted $S$ by raw squared
leans additionally requires control of the residuals. Neither the residual bound nor the
hypotheses (T1), $C=0$ were checked for the historical full-model table.

For the stationary witness in the main proof, let $\alpha=\lambda_b/4$ and
$F=\sum_j f_j^2=\sum_u f_u^2=13h^2/4$. Its residual is $e_{uj}=\alpha f_uf_j$.
The global gradient vanishes because $\sum_jf_j=0$; every reviewer-intercept gradient
vanishes for the same reason. For an item,
$\sum_u e_{uj}=\alpha h f_j=\lambda_b\iota_j$ and
$\sum_u e_{uj}f_u=\alpha Ff_j=\lambda_f f_j$.
For a reviewer, $\sum_j e_{uj}f_j=\alpha Ff_u=\lambda_f f_u$.
These are all coordinate stationarity equations of \eqref{eq:loss}, including the data term.

\subsection{Information computations for \texorpdfstring{\cref{tab:information}}{the information table}}

Ability is integrated against $\mathcal N(0,1)$ with an 80-node Gauss--Hermite rule. For one biased
item, the per-respondent information that no 2PL absorbs is
$\min_{a',b'}\E_\theta\bigl[\mathrm{KL}\bigl(\mathrm{Bern}(m_j(\theta))\,\|\,\mathrm{Bern}(\sig(a'(\theta-b')))\bigr)\bigr]$,
minimized by Nelder--Mead. For a pair it is
$\E_\theta\bigl[I(X_j;X_k\mid\theta)\bigr]$, computed exactly over the four response patterns.
The Kullback--Leibler divergence between the joint mixture and the product of any two 2PLs splits
into this mutual information plus two single-item terms, so the pair column is exactly the part
that no conditionally independent model can absorb. The expected likelihood ratio of a correctly
specified alternative grows as $2N$ times the per-respondent information. For mixtures this is a
heuristic, because the null lies on the boundary of the parameter space~\cite{self1987}.
